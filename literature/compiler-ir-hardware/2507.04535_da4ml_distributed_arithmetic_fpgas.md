# da4ml: Distributed Arithmetic for Real-time Neural Networks on FPGAs

- **Authors:** Chang Sun, Zhiqiang Que, Vladimir Loncar, Wayne Luk, Maria Spiropulu
- **Venue / Year:** ACM Transactions on Reconfigurable Technology and Systems (TRETS), Vol. 19,
  No. 1, Article 13 — submitted 2025-07-06, journal version dated March 2026.
- **arXiv:** 2507.04535 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04535
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04535
- **Code:** https://github.com/calad0i/da4ml (PyPI `da4ml`)
- **Topic area:** compiler / IR / hardware backends

## TL;DR
A standalone library + hls4ml `Strategy='distributed_arithmetic'` backend that replaces
constant-matrix-vector-multiply (CMVM) — i.e. any matmul where one operand is a trained,
synthesis-time-constant weight matrix — with an optimized shift-and-add adder graph using
common subexpression elimination (CSE), emitting either RTL directly or hls4ml-consumable
C++. Explicitly scoped to CMVM only: it does **not** apply to activation×activation matmuls
(e.g. attention's QKᵀ / softmax(QKᵀ)·V), since those have no constant operand to fold.

## Key methods / contributions
- Decomposes each CMVM into a graph of shift/add/subtract ops with CSE, à la the classic
  multiple-constant-multiplication (MCM) literature (Hcub/RAG/ILP lineage), applied for the
  first time at NN-accelerator scale.
- Integrates two ways: (a) standalone, HGQ2 model → da4ml → synthesizable Verilog directly
  (bypasses HLS C-synthesis entirely); (b) as hls4ml's `distributed_arithmetic` Strategy
  (Vitis/Vivado + OneAPI backends only), invoked per-layer, **`reuse_factor` must be 1**.
- Claim: "can reduce on-chip resources by up to a third for realistic, highly quantized
  neural networks," "usually reduce up to 30% of the LUTs and all DSPs" vs. Latency-strategy
  CMVM kernels (paper-stated aggregate claim; not broken out by weight bit-width in what we
  could extract).

## Main results
General quantized-NN benchmarks show LUT reduction "up to a third" and DSP→0. **Not** broken
out for pure ±1 (binary) weight matrices in the parts of the paper we could extract via
WebFetch (PDF text extraction was unreliable; treat any binary-specific number as unverified
pending a direct PDF read).

## Relevance to BNJetTag
This is the mechanism behind our own measured DA experiment (`experiment-log.md`
2026-07-20/07-22): applied to our binary dense layers at RF=1, DA gave a **bit-exact but
resource-NEGATIVE** result — LUT 4.53M vs. 3.91M for plain Latency (**+34%**), FF 7.10M vs.
1.92M (3.7×). Reading: a ±1 weight has exactly one nonzero signed digit already (the trivial
"wire or negate" case Vitis's own LTO already exploits for free), so there is no shift-add
decomposition to share subexpressions *within* a row; da4ml's benefit class (few-bit but
>1-signed-digit constants, e.g. the 6-7-bit heterogeneous weights in the sub-µs-transformer
paper) does not include our binary extreme, and da4ml's deep RTL pipelining registers many
intermediate partial sums (explaining the FF blow-up) with nothing to amortize it against on
an already-minimal per-weight cost. Because DA is fundamentally a CMVM technique, it also
cannot touch our attention core's act×act einsums (QKᵀ, A·V) by construction — those stay on
Latency regardless of DA availability. This paper is the primary source for hls4ml PR #1191
(merged 2025-07-03), which added the `distributed_arithmetic` strategy for
Dense/Conv1D/Conv2D/EinsumDense.
