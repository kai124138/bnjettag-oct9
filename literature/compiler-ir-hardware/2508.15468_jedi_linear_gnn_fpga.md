# JEDI-linear: Fast and Efficient Graph Neural Networks for Jet Tagging on FPGAs

- **Authors:** Zhiqiang Que, Chang Sun, Sudarshan Paramesvaran, et al. (Imperial College
  London, Caltech, University of Bristol, CERN)
- **Venue / Year:** arXiv preprint, August 2025 (v2)
- **arXiv:** 2508.15468 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15468
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15468
- **Topic area:** hls4ml & FPGA triggers / compiler-IR (da4ml application)

## TL;DR
A linear-complexity GNN jet tagger for the CMS/ATLAS L1-trigger-class latency regime that
replaces pairwise Interaction-Network-style edge computation with shared transformations +
global aggregation, and implements **every MAC as multiplier-free distributed arithmetic**
(an extended da4ml with a custom pipelining pass) rather than going through generic hls4ml
Latency/Resource strategies. Directly in our own physics domain (jet tagging, FPGA trigger
latency budgets) and a genuine same-era comparandum for our DA experiment.

## Key methods / contributions
- Fine-grained, per-parameter mixed-precision QAT (via HGQ) — "the majority of non-zero
  weights have less than 3 bits," highly sparse — **not** a fixed binary/ternary constraint
  like BNJetTag's thesis, but adjacent (very-low-bit, weight-sparse regime).
- All MAC operations implemented via **distributed arithmetic**, purely weight×activation
  (CMVM) — no attention or activation×activation product anywhere in the architecture.
- Extended da4ml adds a custom pipelining pass; emits synthesizable Verilog directly
  (bypasses hls4ml C-synthesis), validated with Verilator, placed-and-routed with Vivado 2025.1.

## Main results
Representative row (64 particles, 16 features, AMD VU13P): **latency 93 ns, II = 1 cycle, LUT
192k, DSP 0, FF 92k, Fmax 268.1 MHz.** 128-particle row: latency 110 ns, DSP 0. Headline
comparison claim: 3.7–11.5× lower latency, up to 150× lower II, up to 6.2× lower LUT vs.
state-of-the-art designs (paper's own comparison set, not independently re-verified by us).

## Relevance to BNJetTag
The closest same-domain (CMS/HL-LHC jet tagging), same-latency-class (sub-100-ns, VU13P)
precedent for going straight to a **standalone emitter** (extended da4ml → Verilog) instead
of hls4ml C-synthesis — a genuine existence proof that the "small standalone emitter,
da4ml-style" path (our IR-candidate option (d)) already works in production at trigger
latency for a real HEP tagger, with DSP=0 and II=1. Caveat: their weights are low-bit-but-not-
binary (mixed <3-bit, includes zeros/sparsity) and every op is CMVM (no attention) — the
regime where da4ml/DA is known to help, per its own paper. Does not itself test binary ±1
weights or attention, so it does not settle whether the same custom-pipelining approach would
help or hurt for BNJetTag's specific binary+attention combination — but it is strong evidence
that the *tooling class* (da4ml-derived, standalone RTL emission) is viable at our exact
latency/device target.
