# Lorentz-Equivariant Geometric Algebra Transformers for High-Energy Physics (L-GATr)

- **Authors:** Jonas Spinner, Víctor Bresó, Pim de Haan, Tilman Plehn, Jesse Thaler, Johann Brehmer
- **Venue / Year:** NeurIPS 2024; 2024
- **arXiv:** 2405.14806 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14806
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.14806
- **Code:** https://github.com/heidelberg-hepml/lgatr (also `heidelberg-hepml/lorentz-gatr`)
- **Docs:** https://heidelberg-hepml.github.io/lgatr/
- **Topic area:** Jet tagging & transformers (Lorentz-equivariant geometric-algebra transformer)

## TL;DR
Represents every intermediate quantity as a 16-component **multivector in the geometric algebra
Cl(1,3)** of Minkowski spacetime and builds a Transformer whose core nonlinearity is the
**geometric product between two multivectors that are both learned, data-dependent projections of
the input** — i.e. activation×activation, not weight×activation, at essentially every layer. On
top tagging it is on par with or slightly behind PELICAN, and slightly ahead of ParticleNet/ParT
at matched accuracy metrics, but **the paper never states a parameter count for any model**, and
there is no hardware/FPGA study.

## Summary
L-GATr generalizes the Geometric Algebra Transformer (GATr) idea to the Lorentz group SO(1,3),
replacing standard vector/scalar hidden representations with multivectors so that Lorentz
equivariance is guaranteed by construction (the geometric product and other GA operations are
themselves equivariant maps). The architecture is a genuine Transformer — attention operates over
multivector-valued tokens — making it scalable in a way earlier tensor-representation equivariant
networks (e.g. the Lorentz Group Network, which used explicit finite-dimensional Lorentz
representations and Clebsch-Gordan tensor products) were not. The authors demonstrate it on
regression and classification tasks including jet tagging, and build the first Lorentz-equivariant
generative model (a continuous normalizing flow / Riemannian flow matching) on top of it.

## Key methods / contributions
- **Multivector representation**: every activation is a 16-dimensional object in Cl(1,3)
  (`grade-0` scalar, `grade-1` vector = the physical 4-vector, `grade-2` bivector, `grade-3`
  trivector, `grade-4` pseudoscalar).
- **Geometric Bilinear layer**: the core nonlinear mixing operation. Extracted formula from the
  paper (§3.1, WebFetch of arxiv.org/html/2405.14806v1, accessed 2026-07-24):
  `Linear ∘ GatedGELU ∘ Linear ∘ GP(Linear(x̄), Linear(x̄)) + x`, where `GP(x,y) = xy` is **the
  geometric product defined by Cl(1,3)**, and — critically — **both arguments to `GP` are
  `Linear(x̄)`, i.e. both are learned projections of the same data-dependent activation `x̄`, not
  one weight tensor and one activation.** This is activation×activation by construction, at every
  block, not a one-time or precomputable cost.
- **Equivariant Linear layer**: ordinary weight×activation maps between multivector channels that
  respect the grade structure (these ARE hardware-cheap in isolation) — but they are interleaved
  with the act×act geometric-bilinear layers, not a substitute for them.
- Multivector attention: standard scaled-dot-product attention generalized to operate on
  multivector tokens (also act×act, like ordinary attention, but on 16-wide objects per token).
- The full, unsimplified geometric product of two general Cl(1,3) multivectors is a bilinear map
  `R^16 × R^16 → R^16`; a naive implementation costs up to **16×16 = 256 scalar multiplications**
  per pair of multivectors (before any basis-multiplication-table simplification/cancellation).
  **The paper gives no explicit computational-cost analysis or optimized multiplication count**
  (confirmed absent on direct fetch, accessed 2026-07-24) — treat "≤256 per GP call" as an upper
  bound, not the paper's own claim.

## Main results
Top-tagging accuracy/AUC, compiled from a WebFetch of arxiv.org/html/2405.14806v1 (Table 1,
accessed 2026-07-24) — **cross-check against a PDF read before citing in a paper**, since table
extraction via HTML-to-markdown summarization carries some risk:

| Model | Accuracy | AUC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ParticleNet | 0.9400 | 0.9858 |
| ParT | 0.9400 | 0.9858 |
| L-GATr | 0.9417 ± 0.0002 | 0.9868 ± 0.0001 |
| PELICAN | 0.9426 ± 0.0002 | 0.9870 ± 0.0001 |

PELICAN edges out L-GATr on both metrics here; both edge out ParticleNet/ParT (note: these
particular ParticleNet/ParT numbers are lower than the ones quoted in `2407.08682_miart.md`'s own
run of the same baselines — **0.940/0.9858 here vs. accuracy 0.940/AUC 0.9858 there matches**,
but MIParT's table separately lists a fine-tuned/pretrained ParT at 0.944/0.9877 — different
training protocols across papers are not directly comparable; always check pretrain/fine-tune
status before treating any two "ParT" rows as the same number). **No parameter counts, no
FPGA/latency/resource numbers anywhere in the paper** (confirmed absent on direct fetch).

## Relevance to BNJetTag
L-GATr is the cleanest **REJECT** case in this survey for a binary-weight, DSP-averse FPGA target:
its defining operation (`GP(Linear(x̄), Linear(x̄))`) multiplies two data-dependent activations
together at every block, which is exactly the operation our thesis (binary weights → adder trees)
cannot make free — quantizing or binarizing the *weights* feeding into the two `Linear` calls does
nothing to the `GP` step itself, which needs real fixed-point multipliers (up to 256 per multivector
pair, per channel, per token-pair in attention) regardless of weight precision. Combined with the
complete absence of any parameter count or hardware number in the paper, there is currently **no
evidence L-GATr is viable, or even sized appropriately, for anything resembling our ≤20k-parameter,
sub-µs, VU13P target** — its accuracy edge over ParticleNet/ParT/PELICAN is also marginal (≤0.2 AUC
points) at a Cl(1,3)-multivector-transformer's worth of extra hardware complexity, whereas PELICAN
reaches slightly *better* numbers with a hardware-friendlier operation mix.
