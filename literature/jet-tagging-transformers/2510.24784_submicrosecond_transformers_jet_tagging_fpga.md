# Sub-microsecond Transformers for Jet Tagging on FPGAs

- **Authors:** L. Laatu, C. Sun, A. Cox, A. Gandrakota, B. Maier, J. Ngadiuba, Z. Que, W. Luk,
  M. Spiropulu, A. Tapper (Imperial College London / Caltech / Fermilab)
- **Venue / Year:** NeurIPS 2025 ML4PS workshop (4 pages + refs); FERMILAB-PUB-25-0779-CMS-LDRD
- **arXiv:** 2510.24784 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24784 (v1 only, 26 Oct 2025; no journal version
  as of 2026-07-26)
- **PDF:** `literature/jet-tagging-transformers/2510.24784_submicrosecond_transformers_jet_tagging_fpga.pdf`
- **Topic area:** the closest published *system* to BNJetTag — a transformer jet tagger through
  HGQ2 + hls4ml onto Virtex UltraScale+, on our dataset, at our latency class

## One-line claim

A single-head encoder-only transformer (vanilla MHA or Linformer) can be trained *to* a fixed
350,000-EBOPs hardware budget and synthesized to 202–279k LUT / **0 DSP** / **II = 1** with
O(100) ns latency on an XCU250, beating Deep Sets and MLP-Mixer baselines on the HLS4ML LHC Jet
5-class benchmark.

## What they actually did

- **Architecture (§2.1):** encoder-only, Set-Transformer form, **single attention head**, vanilla
  MHA or Linformer with **K/V projected to k = 2** (Fig. 1 legend, "Linformer proj dim 2"). No
  positional encoding. Input = **(8, 16, 32, 64) particles × 3 features (pT, η, φ)**, pT-sorted.
- **Not stated anywhere in the paper:** d_model, number of encoder blocks, FFN dimension,
  parameter count, clock frequency, FF/BRAM usage, reuse factor, `io_type`, hls4ml `Strategy`,
  and **any mention of softmax at all**. The architecture is not reproducible from the text; the
  only size handle they give is the EBOPs budget. (Our own stack characterization of the code they
  contributed — `QMultiHeadAttention`, table-based `QSoftmax` with exp/inv LUTs — is in
  `bnjettag/results/hgq2/constraints_map.md`, and is more specific than their paper.)
- **Precision (§2.2):** **HGQ** (arXiv:2405.00645) — gradient-based **per-parameter** bitwidths
  *including zero width*, i.e. quantization and pruning in one loss. EBOPs (Σ b_w·b_a over all
  multiplies) is a **term in the training loss**, driven to a target of **350,000 by a PID
  controller on the regularization factor β** (§3). Attention-layer bitwidths are floored at
  **≥ 1 bit** "to disable pruning for training stability"; all other layers may reach 0 bits
  (Fig. 3a vs 3b; distributions span 0–9 bits, with the low-bit mass growing as n grows).
  Additionally: **"value-wise heterogeneous quantization is also applied in the datapath, which
  breaks the otherwise permutation-invariant model to further reduce the firmware footprint"**
  (§2.2, verbatim) — i.e. per-*position* bitwidths, paid for with permutation invariance.
- **Emission (§2.2):** hls4ml native MHA/Linformer (this is the paper that mainlined both), with
  **da4ml** distributed arithmetic on the CMVM operations — exact, no approximation error.
- **Data (§3):** the public HLS4ML LHC Jet dataset, **620,000 train / 260,000 test**, 5 balanced
  classes (g/q/W/Z/t) — the same dataset and the same test-set size as our era-2 ROC-test.
- **Target (§3):** Xilinx **XCU250**, Vitis HLS + Vivado. XCU250 is the Alveo U250's Virtex
  UltraScale+ die — **the same 1,728,000-LUT / 4-SLR silicon class as our VU13P target**
  (this corrects the 2026-07-13 research-log note that called it "NOT VU13P").

## The numbers that matter to us

**Their Table 1** (synthesis; transformer rows only — full table already transcribed in the
research log 2026-07-02):

| Model | Particles | Acc. (%) | Latency (ns) | LUT (k) | II (clk) | DSP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MHA | 8 / 16 / 32 | 66.3 / 72.3 / 77.0 | 104 / 98 / 83 | 246 / 279 / 180 | 1 | 0 |
| MHA | 64 | 77.9 | 44 | 47 | 1 | 0 |
| Linformer | 8 / 16 / 32 / 64 | 66.3 / 72.8 / 78.4 / 79.8 | 110 / 103 / 140 / 78 | 230 / 246 / 267 / 202 | 1 | 0 |

**Three caveats that only appear in the body, not the abstract:**

1. **The MHA 64-particle row is not a transformer.** §3, verbatim: "The attention block of the MHA
   model with 64 input particles is consistently collapsing over several trained models despite the
   bitwidth constrained to at least one bit, **turning it into a Deep Set**, which explains its
   vastly different resource usage." The 47k-LUT / 44 ns number must never be quoted as an
   attention result.
2. **Table 1 is an iso-resource sweep, not a scaling study.** MHA LUT *falls* 279k → 180k → 47k as
   n goes 16 → 32 → 64, because the EBOPs target is held fixed and the optimizer spends the budget
   on bits. Fig. 1's caption says so outright: the accuracy drop at 64 particles "is expected due
   to the fixed resource budget we enforced during training." Nothing here tells us how a
   transformer's resources scale with anything.
3. **The synthesis stage is never stated in this paper.** §3 says only "All experiments are
   performed using Vitis HLS and Vivado." Their MLP-Mixer baseline rows are lifted from ref. [18]
   (Sun et al., arXiv:2503.03103, Mach. Learn. Sci. Technol. 2025), which *does* state its
   convention: XCVU9P @ **200 MHz**, Vitis HLS 2023.2 + Vivado 2023.2, and **"Resource consumption
   is reported post place & route"** (verbatim, arXiv HTML v2, accessed 2026-07-26). Same lead
   author (C. Sun), same year, same tools. **Inference, not a stated fact: Table 1's LUTs are
   almost certainly post-P&R, not csynth estimates.**

**Accuracy (Fig. 2, per-class one-vs-rest AUC).** Macro-averages below are *our* arithmetic mean of
their five per-class numbers, not a number the paper states:

| n | model | g | q | W | Z | t | macro-avg (ours) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | Linformer | 0.853 | 0.886 | 0.917 | 0.900 | 0.929 | **0.897** |
| 8 | MHA | 0.854 | 0.886 | 0.916 | 0.900 | 0.929 | **0.897** |
| 64 | Linformer | 0.941 | 0.921 | 0.972 | 0.968 | 0.964 | **0.953** |

**Is this comparable to our 0.8902?** On the metric axis, **yes** — same public dataset, same
260,000-jet test set, same one-vs-rest AUC construction, so a macro average of their five numbers
is the same object as our era-2 ROC-test macro-OvR AUC. On the model axis, **no** — they use 3
features/particle to our 16, and their nearest sequence length (8) is not our 10. Also unstated:
whether Fig. 2 is the quantized or the float model. Read as: **at ~our sequence length, a
3-feature heterogeneously-quantized Linformer reaches ≈0.897, which sits above our binary flagship
(0.8902, `r7/roc-results/r8/roc_auc_r8.md`) and below our own FP32 (0.9231, RESEARCH.md §2).**

**The fit gap, stated iso-II** (COMPILER.md's own rule; never compare across II):

| | LUT | DSP | II | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Their MHA-8 | 246k | 0 | 1 | their Table 1, probably post-P&R |
| Their Linformer-8 | 230k | 0 | 1 | their Table 1, probably post-P&R |
| Our stdnn d32 flagship | **4.80M** | **14,112** | 1 | `r7/results/activation_ladder.md`, **csynth estimate** |
| Our stdnn d32 flagship | 3.91M | 1,764 | 8 | same |

At matched II = 1 on the same silicon class we are **≈20× their LUT and 14,112 DSP to their 0** —
but the two sides are measured at different stages, which is exactly the problem (below).

## The governing question: how do they fit where we do not

Their fit is bought **entirely on the precision axis at full spatial parallelism**. Every model in
their Table 1 is II = 1; **they fold nothing**. The mechanism, in order of force:

1. **They train to the budget instead of measuring it after the fact.** EBOPs in the loss + a PID
   controller on β means the hardware target is a *constraint*, not an outcome. This is the whole
   trick, and Fig. 1/Table 1 are what it looks like: accuracy varies, resources don't.
2. **Per-parameter bitwidths with zero-width pruning**, floored at 1 bit only inside attention.
3. **Per-value/per-position widths**, explicitly trading permutation invariance for footprint.
4. **da4ml adder graphs** for every CMVM.
5. **Linformer k = 2**, killing the O(n²) act×act core.
6. **3 features per particle**, single head, no positional encoding.

**Is this mechanism available to us? Mostly not, by construction — and that is the finding.**

- Our EBOPs at W1A8 for the d32/H4/L2/FFN64 flagship is ≈**2.18M** (our arithmetic from the
  shape: 170,144 weight MACs × 1 × 8 + 12,800 act×act MACs × 8 × 8; the 12,800 reproduces
  RESEARCH.md §2 exactly) — **6.2× their 350k budget**. But HGQ's EBOPs **excludes the accumulator
  by definition** (quoted verbatim in `code/training/ebops.py`), and for a ±1-weight design the
  adder tree *is* the entire cost. So (a) EBOPs systematically **flatters** our design, and (b)
  minimizing EBOPs with b_w already pinned at 1 collapses to minimizing activation bits — which is
  our **closed** `activation-width-as-primary-lever`. Their optimizer would have nothing left to
  search for us.
- Levers 2 and 3 are **incompatible with token-axis folding**, which requires the 10 per-token
  instances to be *identical* (COMPILER.md §5: "13 of 15 weighted layers exist as 10 identical
  instances"). Per-value widths destroy that identity. They are also incompatible with the thesis.
- Lever 4 is **closed and measured negative** for us (2026-07-22: +16% LUT, ×3.7 FF — the CSD of a
  ±1 weight is one digit, so its CSE has nothing to share).
- Lever 5 is **live and untried** (below).

The honest one-sentence answer: **they fit because they spend the precision axis, and we cannot,
because pinning weights to {−1,+1} spends that axis before training starts — leaving the schedule
axis (reuse over tokens) as the only budget we still own.** That is COMPILER.md's thesis, and this
paper is the strongest available evidence for it.

## What we can steal

1. **Run one whole-model Vivado `synth_design` before anything else.** This is the highest-value
   action the paper implies, and it is cheap. Their numbers are near-certainly post-P&R; our 3.91M
   is a csynth estimate; our own single-layer anchor measured csynth over-estimating LUT by
   **2.29–2.51×** (`results/adder-graph/e1/POSTSYN.md`, unregistered, xczu7ev). **3.91M ÷ 2.3–2.5
   = 1.56–1.70M, i.e. under the 1,728,000 VU13P budget.** The entire "2.26× over" framing may be a
   measurement-stage artifact. `xcvu13p` is unlicensed on mulder but `xcvu7ev` synthesizes
   out-of-context and reports >100% utilization without failing.
2. **Linformer k = 2 on the act×act core.** Not on our closed list; present in our stack only as a
   compat shim (`bnhgq2/compat.py`, `QLinformerAttention`), never trained or synthesized. At n=10,
   k=2 cuts act×act MACs 5× (12,800 → 2,560) and softmax logits 10× (800 → 80), and it converts
   act×act products — the only DSP consumers we have — into **constant-weight** products, i.e. into
   ±1 adder trees. Their evidence that it is accuracy-free: at n=8, Linformer and MHA tie *exactly*
   (66.3%/66.3%, macro-AUC 0.897/0.897), and Linformer ≥ MHA at every larger n (Fig. 1). Post-E-C
   this is worth ~100k LUT, not a fit lever — its real value is **whole-model DSP = 0**, which is
   their headline and currently not ours.
3. **Train to a cost target, not to a checkpoint.** The PID-on-β control loop is a methodology we
   do not have. Substitute our own cost model (the per-token-slice LUT census) for EBOPs, since
   EBOPs is the wrong currency for ±1 (above). This is the one *methodological* import worth a
   round.
4. **A baseline we should run:** their Linformer/MHA at n=8, 3 features, is a legitimate iso-dataset
   external reference point for §2 — quotable as *their* number, never next to ours without the
   feature-count caveat.

## What threatens us

1. **"First transformer for jet tagging on FPGA" is taken** (their abstract, Oct 2025). Our claim
   must be "first *binary-weight* transformer" and/or the compiler mechanism — not first, not
   sub-µs, not "transformers fit in L1."
2. **DSP = 0 is taken, and it is not binary-specific.** Every HGQ-trained model in their Table 1 —
   including **Deep Sets and MLP-Mixer**, which are not transformers — reports 0 DSP at every n.
   Worse, they get whole-model 0 DSP *including attention*, where our flagship spends 1,764 DSP at
   II=8 (14,112 at II=1) on the act×act core. **On the DSP axis our current story is weaker than
   theirs, not stronger.** The thesis must be re-pitched on the *mechanism* (uniform ±1 → one
   adder-tree topology, no per-weight descriptors, no da4ml search, foldable instances) rather than
   on the DSP=0 outcome.
3. **"Binary is a strictly worse special case of learned mixed precision."** Fig. 3 shows HGQ
   already discovers ~1-bit weights where they pay off and 0-bit (pruned) weights elsewhere, at
   better accuracy than our binary flagship on the same data. The defensible answer is structural,
   and this paper supplies the evidence for it: their per-value heterogeneous widths make instances
   non-identical, so **nothing in their design can be folded** — every row is II=1. Uniform ±1 is
   what makes reuse legal.
4. **The clock is a hidden constraint we may be underestimating.** Their sibling paper closes
   timing at **200 MHz (5 ns)** on a VU9P with the same tool versions. Our entire II ≤ 11 budget
   rests on a **2.157 ns csynth estimate** plus a single-layer 2.5 ns post-synthesis anchor. If the
   folded whole model only closes at 200 MHz, II=10 → 50 ns/jet, **2× over the 25 ns envelope**,
   and the fold campaign's II ceiling drops to ~5 — halving the fold gain and making the FR
   restructuring pass mandatory rather than optional. Achieved clock must stay a first-class output.
5. **Their smaller-input model matches our binary AUC at ~1/20 the LUT.** ≈0.897 (3 features, n=8,
   230k LUT post-P&R, II=1) vs our 0.8902 (16 features, n=10, 4.80M LUT csynth, II=1). Say it
   plainly rather than hiding behind the feature-count difference.

## How we'd cite it

> Sub-microsecond transformer jet taggers have been demonstrated on Virtex UltraScale+ with
> heterogeneously quantized attention trained to a fixed ~350k-EBOPs budget, reaching O(100) ns
> latency at II = 1 with zero DSPs [Laatu et al., arXiv:2510.24784]; that design buys device fit
> entirely on the precision axis at full spatial parallelism, which a uniform {−1,+1} weight
> constraint forecloses — we therefore buy fit on the schedule axis, by time-multiplexing one
> constant ±1 datapath over the token sequence, a transform their per-value heterogeneous
> bitwidths make illegal.

**Verdict: essential.** The only same-dataset, same-metric, same-silicon-class, same-toolchain
system in the literature. It is a rival on "a transformer fits in L1" and a supporting precedent
for "aggressive quantization removes DSPs"; the paper's own body (attention collapse at n=64,
iso-EBOPs table, unstated architecture) is where its numbers become quotable rather than
intimidating.
