# Investigating 1-Bit Quantization in Transformer-Based Top Tagging (BitParT)

- **Authors:** Saurabh Rai, Prisha, Jitendra Kumar (I.I.T. Jodhpur, India)
- **Venue / Year:** arXiv preprint; 2025
- **arXiv:** 2508.07431 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07431
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07431
- **Topic area:** BitNet & 1-bit/ternary quantization × Jet tagging & transformers (the closest
  published prior art to BNJetTag's own thesis — a partially-binarized Particle Transformer)

## TL;DR
Applies **BitLinear** (the BitNet-style ternary/binary linear layer) to a ParT-family transformer,
but **only inside the feed-forward network and the final dense classifier** — attention (all
Q/K/V/MHA), LayerNorm, the pairwise-interaction embedding network (PairEmbed / ParT's U), and the
per-particle input MLPs all stay full precision. With ~67% of weights (by count) binarized,
accuracy and AUC barely move (0.940→0.9399 accuracy, 0.9862→0.9856 AUC) but **background rejection
drops 15–17%** at fixed signal efficiency — a reminder that AUC/accuracy parity can hide a real
degradation visible only in the low-FPR operating regime. **No FPGA/hardware/latency numbers are
reported anywhere; the authors explicitly flag this as unfinished work.**

## Summary
This is, as far as this survey found, the only published attempt to binarize any part of a
Particle-Transformer-family network. The authors' own framing is explicitly about "maintain[ing]
sensitivity to physically meaningful correlations" (quoted) by leaving attention and the
interaction-feature pathway untouched, and binarizing only the parts of the network they consider
generic/compressible (FFN, classifier head). The result is a mild accuracy trade for meaningful
model-size compression, evaluated purely as a compression study — there is no hardware synthesis,
no resource count, and no argument connecting the binarization choice to DSP/LUT savings.

## Key methods / contributions
- **BitLinear** applied only to: "all weights within the feed-forward networks and the dense
  classifier layers" (quoted, WebFetch of arxiv.org/html/2508.07431v1, accessed 2026-07-24).
- **Full precision, unchanged:** all `MultiheadAttention` operations (i.e. Q/K/V projections and
  the attention computation itself), LayerNorm, the **PairEmbed module** (their name for ParT's
  interaction-matrix network — i.e. **U itself is never binarized or even touched in this paper**),
  and the per-particle input-embedding MLPs.
- Evaluated on the **standard Top Quark Tagging Reference Dataset**, using "the leading 200
  constituent particles" per jet (quoted) — binary top/QCD classification, not our 5-class
  HLS4ML LHC Jet dataset, and 200 constituents vs. our 10.
- **No stated hardware/latency rationale** for the full-precision/binarized split — the paper
  gives a physics-fidelity justification only, not a resource-cost one, and explicitly states:
  *"this work has not yet quantified inference latency or energy consumption improvements"*
  (quoted, accessed 2026-07-24).

## Main results
WebFetch extraction of arxiv.org/html/2508.07431v1 (accessed 2026-07-24):

| Metric | ParT (their reimplementation) | BitParT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | 0.940 | 0.9399 |
| AUC | 0.9862 | 0.9856 |
| Background rejection @ ε_S=50% | 429 ± 20 | 366 ± 17 |
| Background rejection @ ε_S=30% | 1570 ± 130 | 1301 ± 106 |

Both models: **~1.23M trainable parameters** (BitLinear reduces effective storage/compute per
weight, not parameter *count*). Over 67% of weights are binarized by their count. Their own
framing: "15% reduction in [background] rejection" at 50% efficiency alongside "less than 0.15%"
AUC difference — i.e. **AUC moved by 0.0006 while background rejection moved by ~15–17%**, a
concrete illustration that AUC is not a sensitive-enough metric to catch a real quantization
degradation in the operating regime (low false-positive rate) that actually matters for a trigger.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
This is the single closest piece of published prior art to our own project's core move — but it
differs from us on essentially every axis that matters for our thesis, which both limits its
value as a direct comparison and leaves the harder half of our problem (binarizing attention +
the interaction pathway, and doing it on hardware) completely untouched by the literature:
(1) **partial, not full, binarization** — attention and any interaction-feature pathway are
excluded, whereas our own project's open question (already flagged in
`.claude/memory/research-log.md`'s BinaryAttention entry, 2026-07-02) is exactly whether the
attention core itself can be binarized; (2) **no hardware synthesis at all** — zero LUT/DSP/latency
numbers, vs. our hls4ml/Vitis pipeline being the whole point; (3) **different dataset/task** (binary
top-tagging, 200 constituents) vs. ours (5-class, 10 constituents); (4) **different scale** (1.23M
params vs. our 18.4k). The most transferable finding is methodological, not architectural: **track
background rejection at fixed low efficiency alongside AUC when evaluating any further activation
or interaction-feature quantization** — a 0.06% AUC move hid a 15–17% rejection move here, and our
own ROC-test macro-OvR AUC gap reporting (FP32 0.9161 → binary 0.8902) should be read alongside
the equivalent low-FPR/high-purity operating point, not AUC alone, when judging any physics-native
architecture addition.
