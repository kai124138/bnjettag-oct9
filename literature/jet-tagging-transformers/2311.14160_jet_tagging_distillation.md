# Efficient and Robust Jet Tagging at the LHC with Knowledge Distillation

- **Authors:** R. Liu (UC Berkeley), A. Gandrakota, J. Ngadiuba (Fermilab), M. Spiropulu,
  J.-R. Vlimant (Caltech)
- **Venue / Year:** ML4PS workshop, NeurIPS 2023 (4 pages + refs); FERMILAB-PUB-23-748-CMS
- **arXiv:** 2311.14160 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14160 (v1 only, 23 Nov 2023; no journal
  version and no revision as of 2026-07-26)
- **Code:** https://github.com/ryanliu30/KD4Jets.git (hyperlink extracted from the PDF; **not
  inspected this session**)
- **PDF:** `literature/jet-tagging-transformers/2311.14160_jet_tagging_distillation.pdf`
- **Topic area:** the only in-domain (jet tagging) knowledge-distillation paper with published
  numbers; read as the prior for the planned KD arm on the d32 binary student

## One-line claim

Distilling a Lorentz-equivariant GNN teacher (LorentzNet) into small, weak-inductive-bias
students improves top-tagging performance at zero inference cost — up to +1.5 accuracy points
and ~2× background rejection for an MLP student — and additionally transfers the teacher's
Lorentz-boost robustness to a student never trained to be boost-robust.

## What they actually did

- **Task / data (§3.1):** the **top tagging reference dataset** (Kasieczka et al.) — **binary**,
  top-quark jets vs light-quark/gluon jets, Pythia8 + Delphes. **1.2M train / 400K val /
  400K test.** Inputs are constituent four-momenta in cylindrical coordinates
  **(pT, η, φ, m)** — 4 features/particle, with the *true* particle mass appended so the model
  can distinguish species (§3.2).
- **Teacher (§3.3):** **LorentzNet** [Gong et al. 2022], a Lorentz-equivariant message-passing
  GNN, **224K params, 339M FLOPs**. Taken **pre-trained, off the shelf** — not retrained
  (§6 "we used a pre-trained model as the teacher").
- **Students (§3.2), both trained from scratch and with KD:**
  - **DeepSet:** Zaheer-style `f(X) = ρ(Σ ϕ(x))`; ρ and ϕ each a **3-layer MLP, hidden 128**,
    batch-norm after leaky-ReLU; EFN-style pT-weighted aggregation for IR safety.
    **68.2K params, 1.67M FLOPs.**
  - **MLP:** particles pT-sorted, trimmed/zero-padded to 128 → **4 × 128 = 512 input
    features** → **3 layers × 512 hidden**, leaky-ReLU + batch-norm. **527K params,
    529K FLOPs.**
- **KD recipe (Eq. 2), verbatim:** `L_KD(q; p, y) = (1 − λ) H(y, q) + λ D_KL(p̃ ‖ q̃)` — pure
  **logit-level** Hinton KD (CE on hard labels + KL against the temperature-softened teacher).
  **There is no feature-level, attention-map, or intermediate-representation term anywhere in
  the paper.**
- **Training (§3.3):** 100 epochs, **AdamW**, **StepLR**. Temperatures **T ∈ {1, 3, 5}**.
  No KD warm-up, no λ or T annealing, **no schedule of any kind on the loss mix**.
- **Quantization: none. FPGA/hls4ml: none.** The word "quantization" does not appear. The only
  concession to hardware is that they report FLOPs (`fvcore`, torch-jit tracing) "with an eye to
  deployment to real-time system" (§3.4). **No synthesis, no latency, no resource numbers.**

**Two recipe holes that make the headline result non-reproducible from the text:**

1. **λ is never stated for the Table-1 experiment.** The only λ in the paper is **λ = 1** in the
   *second* (Lorentz-robustness) experiment, chosen there "to prevent the model from learning
   from the ground truth labels" (§3.3). The mixing weight behind every number in Table 1 is
   absent. Only the repo could settle it.
2. **Eq. 2 carries no T² factor on the KL term.** Hinton's formulation rescales the soft-target
   gradient by T² so that varying T does not silently vary the effective KD weight. As written,
   their T-scan is **confounded with an effective-λ scan** — the T = 1 → 3 → 5 trend cannot be
   read as a pure temperature effect. (Whether the code applies T² is unverified.)

## The numbers that matter to us

**Their Table 1** (400K-jet test set, **binary** top-vs-QCD; `Rej_X% ≡ 1/FPR at TPR = X%`).
One run per row — **no seeds, no error bars anywhere in the paper**:

| Model | #params | FLOPs | Accuracy | AUC | Rej30% | Rej50% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DeepSet from scratch | 68.2K | 1.67M | 0.930 | 0.9808 | 747 | 219 |
| DeepSet KD T = 1 | " | " | 0.932 | 0.9818 | 926 | 241 |
| DeepSet KD T = 3 | " | " | 0.932 | **0.9819** | **970** | **255** |
| DeepSet KD T = 5 | " | " | 0.932 | **0.9819** | **970** | 248 |
| MLP from scratch | 527K | 529K | 0.904 | 0.9663 | 256 | 82 |
| MLP KD T = 1 | " | " | 0.914 | 0.9726 | 375 | 119 |
| MLP KD T = 3 | " | " | 0.918 | 0.9751 | 483 | 144 |
| MLP KD T = 5 | " | " | **0.919** | 0.9750 | **503** | **146** |
| **LorentzNet (teacher)** | 224K | 339M | 0.942 | 0.9868 | 2195 | 498 |

**The single most important line in the paper for us, and it is not in the abstract.** The
conclusion (§4) headlines "a 25% improvement in background rejection"; that is the **DeepSet**
Rej30% row (747 → 970 is in fact **+29.9%**; 747 → 926 at T = 1 is +24.0%). **The AUC gain
behind that same 25–30% rejection improvement is +0.0011, and the accuracy gain is +0.002.**
KD moved the operating-point metric by ~30% while moving AUC by one part in a thousand. The
abstract, notably, oversells nothing — it claims only "an overall boost"; the 25% figure lives
in §4.

**Their AUC is not our AUC.** Theirs is a **2-class** top-vs-QCD AUC on a 400K-jet Delphes test
set in the 0.97–0.99 regime; ours is **5-class macro-OvR** on the public HLS4ML LHC Jet ROC-test
(n = 260,000) at 0.89–0.94 (`RESEARCH.md` §2). Their `Rej30%/Rej50%` are single-class binary
rejections in the hundreds; our rejection @ε_S = 0.5 is a 5-class macro number of order 30–100.
**No number from their Table 1 may be placed beside one of ours.** What transfers is the
*shape* of the result, and only through the three scale-free bridges below.

**Three bridges from their gains to our +0.005 target** (all arithmetic below is **ours**, not
stated by the paper; our numbers from `RESEARCH.md` §2 round-11 ladder: d32 W1A8-stdnn **0.8902
± 0.0067**, d128 W1A8-stdnn teacher **0.9335**, d128 W8A8-stdnn **0.9380**):

| bridge | DeepSet | MLP | implied for our d32 binary student |
|---|---|---|---|
| raw ΔAUC | **+0.0011** | **+0.0088** | our target +0.005 sits between the two |
| fraction of the teacher–student AUC gap closed | 0.0011 / 0.0060 = **18%** | 0.0088 / 0.0205 = **43%** | ×0.0433 gap → **+0.0079 to +0.0186** |
| relative reduction in (1 − AUC) | 0.0192→0.0181 = **5.7%** | 0.0337→0.0249 = **26%** | ×0.1098 → **+0.0063 to +0.0287** |

Our +0.005 target is **4.6%** of our (1 − AUC), i.e. *less* demanding in error-area terms than
either of their students, and it is **below** the range implied by the gap-closing bridge. On
the numbers alone the target looks conservative. The mechanisms below are why that is not the
whole story.

**Compression ratio — the paper has no data point at our ratio, and the axis is FLOPs, not
params.** Our arm is **14.85× in parameters** (285,121 → 19,201) and ≈**15× in MACs** (our
arithmetic: d32 flagship ≈183k MACs = 170,144 weight + 12,800 act×act; d128 at H4/L2/FFN=2d
scales to ≈2.8M). Theirs:

| | teacher→student params | teacher→student FLOPs |
|---|---|---|
| DeepSet | 224K → 68.2K = **3.3×** | 339M → 1.67M = **203×** |
| MLP | 224K → 527K = **0.43× (student is 2.4× LARGER)** | 339M → 529K = **641×** |

Read this carefully: **their larger AUC gain came from a student with more parameters than the
teacher.** Their compression is entirely a compute story. On the *parameter* axis we are 4.5×
more aggressive than their best-matched case; on the *FLOP* axis we are 13–43× **milder** than
either of theirs.

## What we can steal

1. **Pre-register background rejection at fixed ε_S as a co-primary endpoint, not AUC alone.**
   This is the strongest, cheapest import. Their DeepSet — the student whose situation most
   resembles ours — bought **+30% Rej30% for +0.0011 AUC**. An arm gated on "+0.005 macro-OvR
   AUC" would have scored that as a failure. We already report rejection @ε_S = 0.3/0.5/0.7
   throughout `RESEARCH.md` §2, so this costs nothing but a decision made *before* the runs.
2. **T ∈ {1, 3, 5}, and stop there.** Gains saturate by T = 3 in both students (DeepSet AUC
   0.9819 at both T = 3 and T = 5; MLP 0.9751 vs 0.9750). T = 1 is materially worse for the
   MLP (+0.0063 vs +0.0088) and nearly equivalent for the DeepSet. Do **not** copy "T = 3 is
   optimal" — their T = 3 vs T = 5 separation (Rej50% 255 vs 248) is one seed and is noise.
3. **Add the λ = 1 arm as a diagnostic**, which is what they used for experiment 2. Pure KL with
   the hard labels switched off answers a question we actually have: can the teacher's soft
   targets alone train a {−1,+1} student better than hard labels can, i.e. is soft-target
   smoothing compensating for STE gradient noise? It is one extra run and it isolates the
   dark-knowledge channel from the CE channel.
4. **Decide λ ourselves and sweep it — the paper gives us nothing here.** Since λ is unstated
   for Table 1 and Eq. 2 has no T² correction, treat (λ, T) as a **coupled** 2-D choice. Either
   implement the T² rescaling and scan T cleanly, or reproduce their un-scaled form and accept
   that T is a re-parameterization of λ. Pick one, write it down.
5. **Run their overfitting diagnostic before assuming their mechanism (Fig. 2b).** Their MLP's
   validation loss *rises* without KD and *converges* with it — a substantial part of their
   largest gain is regularization. Our 19,201-param student on 620,000 training jets is almost
   certainly nowhere near overfitting; if our training curves confirm that, this mechanism is
   unavailable and the expected gain should be revised downward on the spot.
6. **State that KD is free at inference.** They measure FLOPs precisely to make this point.
   For us it is stronger: the KD arm changes **no** LUT, DSP, II, or latency number — it is the
   only accuracy lever we have that does not compete with the device budget.
7. **A teacher choice the paper licenses:** §5 explicitly endorses **cross-architecture**
   distillation (GNN → deep set, GNN → MLP both worked). Nothing obliges our teacher to be a
   d128 member of the student's own family. A stronger non-transformer teacher is on the table.

## What threatens us

1. **It does not scoop us and it does not contradict the DSP = 0 story** — no quantization, no
   FPGA, no synthesis, different dataset, 2 classes. **The threat is entirely to the KD arm's
   expected effect size, not to the thesis.**
2. **The paper's two claimed mechanisms are both unavailable to us, and this is the finding.**
   (a) *Inductive-bias transfer* (§2.2, §3.3, their headline novelty): the teacher has Lorentz
   equivariance the students structurally lack. Our planned teacher is a **d128 member of the
   same architecture family** — same features, same T = 10, same attention — so there is no
   inductive bias to transfer. (b) *Regularization against overfitting* (Fig. 2b): our student
   is 19.2k params on 620k jets. Strip both and what remains for us is only the class-relationship
   prior + gradient rescaling of Tang et al. [17], which they cite but never isolate.
3. **Their well-matched student got +0.0011 AUC.** The DeepSet is the closer analogue to our
   situation (correct inductive bias, genuinely smaller than the teacher) and its AUC gain is
   **4.5× smaller than our +0.005 target — at a 3.3× compression ratio rather than 15×.** If a
   referee knows this paper, "KD gives us +0.005 AUC" is not supported by the only in-domain
   precedent, and we should not write that sentence.
4. **Our teacher–student gap is larger than anything they tested.** Theirs: 0.60 AUC pts
   (DeepSet) and 2.05 pts (MLP). Ours: **4.33 pts** to the d128 binary teacher, **4.78 pts** to
   d128 W8A8-stdnn. The gap-closing bridge above is the optimistic reading; the pessimistic one
   is the known capacity-gap failure mode of KD, on which this paper offers **zero** evidence.
   An intermediate-teacher (d48/d64, already trained in round 11) fallback should be designed in
   from the start.
5. **The target is smaller than our seed noise, and the paper cannot help with that** — it has
   no seeds at all. Our student's spread is ±0.0067 (`RESEARCH.md` §2). Unpaired, resolving
   +0.005 at ~2σ needs **≈14 seeds per arm** (our arithmetic: σ√(2/n) ≤ 0.0025). Paired seeds
   plus a paired test-set bootstrap on the shared 260,000-jet ROC-test is the only affordable
   route; **uncertainty-analyst must set the design before launch, not after.**
6. **Nothing in the literature — this paper included — says anything about KD × binarization.**
   Every model here is FP32. The interaction that matters most to us (does a soft target help or
   hurt when the student's weights are STE-binarized, and does KD fight or assist the latent-weight
   dynamics) is unmeasured in-domain. That is an opportunity as much as a risk, but it means our
   arm is **not** a replication — it is a new measurement, and should be scoped as one.

## How we'd cite it

> Knowledge distillation has been shown to improve small jet taggers at fixed inference cost,
> with a Lorentz-equivariant teacher raising an MLP student's top-tagging accuracy by 1.5 points
> and its background rejection by roughly a factor of two [Liu et al., arXiv:2311.14160]; those
> gains were obtained in full precision and were driven largely by transferring an inductive bias
> the student lacked, whereas our student shares its teacher's architecture and differs only in
> width and weight precision — so we report the effect of distillation on a binary-weight student
> as a distinct measurement, gated on background rejection as well as AUC.

**Verdict: useful.** In-domain, honest, and the correct prior to hold — but it is a 4-page
workshop note with one seed per cell, an unstated λ, no quantization, no hardware, a 2-class
dataset, and no data point at our compression ratio. Its most valuable contribution to our arm
is not its AUC gains but its **decoupling of AUC from rejection** (+0.0011 vs +30%), which
should change what we pre-register. Treat its Table 1 as a shape, never as a number next to ours.
