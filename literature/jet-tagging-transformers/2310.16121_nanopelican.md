# 19 Parameters Is All You Need: Tiny Neural Networks for Particle Physics (nanoPELICAN)

- **Authors:** Alexander Bogatskiy, Timothy Hoffman, Jan T. Offermann
- **Venue / Year:** NeurIPS 2023 Workshop on Machine Learning and the Physical Sciences; 2023
  (final version dated 2023-12-13)
- **arXiv:** 2310.16121 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16121
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16121
- **Topic area:** Jet tagging & transformers (extreme-small-parameter Lorentz-equivariant
  aggregator — direct follow-up to PELICAN)

## TL;DR
Strips PELICAN (see `2211.00454_pelican.md`) down to its symmetry-irreducible minimum: **two
linear permutation-equivariant blocks separated by a single ReLU**, reaching a **21-parameter**
(19 after algebraic absorption) top-tagging model with **AUC 0.9718** — beating a 59,000-parameter
plain MLP (TopoDNN, AUC 0.964) by nearly 3,000× fewer parameters, and a 101-parameter variant
trailing full ParticleNet (498k params) and ParT (2.1M params) by only ~1% AUC. This is the
single most extreme accuracy-per-parameter existence proof found in this survey — but it is
floating-point, GPU-only, with no hardware/latency study.

## Summary
The paper asks how far PELICAN's parameter count can be pushed down before performance
collapses, by (a) using only 80 (of up to ~200) constituents per jet, ranked by pT, (b) reducing
PELICAN's 15-function equivariant-aggregation basis to the **6 functions that are not redundant
once diagonal-only and index-symmetric duplicates are removed**, and (c) collapsing the network to
a single hidden nonlinearity. The result, "nanoPELICAN," has a parameter count in the tens, not
thousands, while still substantially outperforming non-equivariant baselines with orders of
magnitude more parameters.

## Key methods / contributions
- **Input:** N×N matrix of pairwise Minkowski dot products `d_ij = p_i·p_j`, truncated to the
  top-80-pT constituents ("typically enough to saturate our network's performance" — quoted,
  arXiv:2310.16121, accessed 2026-07-24). Forming this matrix is treated as **preprocessing, not
  counted in the parameter budget.**
- **Architecture:** exactly `LinEq2→2 → ReLU → LinEq2→0` — one hidden nonlinearity total.
  `LinEq2→2^nano` uses only 6 (of PELICAN's original 15) basis aggregators, after removing 5 that
  depend only on the diagonal and one from each of 4 symmetric-duplicate pairs. `LinEq2→0` uses
  just 2 aggregators (total sum, trace).
- **Parameter formula** (their own, after absorbing ReLU's positive homogeneity into adjacent
  linear layers): `9·C_hidden + 4` for `C_hidden > 1` (12 for `C_hidden = 1`).
- A second variant, **nPELICAN_N**, adds explicit N-dependent rescaling of the aggregators
  (rather than learning the `(N/N̄)^α` factor as in full PELICAN), trading a handful of extra
  parameters for a small accuracy gain.

## Main results
Both from arXiv:2310.16121 (WebFetch of arxiv.org/html, accessed 2026-07-24), top-tagging
reference dataset, 80-constituent truncation:

| Model (Table 1) | Accuracy | AUC | 1/ε_B | Params |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LorentzNet (n_hidden=3, authors' own tiny reimplementation — **not** the original 220k model) | 0.907(2) | 0.966(3) | 174±44 | 120 |
| nPELICAN (C_hidden=10) | 0.921(1) | 0.9748(1) | 327±20 | 101 |
| nPELICAN (C_hidden=3) | 0.919(1) | 0.9730(4) | 256±12 | 31 |
| nPELICAN (C_hidden=2) | 0.918(1) | 0.9718(6) | 243±18 | **21** |
| nPELICAN (C_hidden=1) | 0.895(1) | 0.950(2) | 81±12 | 11 |

| Model (Table 2, N-scaled aggregators) | Accuracy | AUC | 1/ε_B | Params |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| nPELICAN_N (C_hidden=10) | 0.923(1) | 0.9764(1) | 448±10 | 108 |
| nPELICAN_N (C_hidden=3) | 0.9214(3) | 0.9752(2) | 384±16 | 38 |
| nPELICAN_N (C_hidden=2) | 0.9200(3) | 0.9745(1) | 368±17 | 28 |
| nPELICAN_N (C_hidden=1) | 0.902(2) | 0.960(2) | 150±16 | 18 |

Baselines quoted in the same paper: **TopoDNN (59k params): AUC 0.964(14), accuracy 0.929(1)** —
beaten outright by the 21-param nPELICAN. **ParticleNet (498k params) and ParT (2.1M params)**
lead the 101-param nPELICAN_N by only "~1% AUC" (qualitative statement, exact ParticleNet/ParT
numbers on this specific truncated-input setup not independently re-extracted this session — use
the values in `1902.08570_particlenet.md`/`2202.03772_particle_transformer.md` provenance only for
the *general* ParticleNet/ParT numbers, not this paper's exact comparison run).

**No FPGA, no latency, no quantization.** Explicitly GPU-only: "training took about 30 ms per
batch, and the evaluation took about 23 ms per batch... on an Nvidia H100 GPU" — the authors
motivate the work by trigger-latency concerns (**"particle collisions...happen every 25
nanoseconds"**, quoted) but never close the loop with an actual hardware synthesis.

## Relevance to BNJetTag
This is the closest published data point to our own scale (18.4k params) from the
Lorentz-equivariant side, and it is dramatically smaller than us (11–120 params, ~150–1,700× under
our budget) while still beating a 59k-param unstructured MLP baseline. It is strong evidence that
**structure buys accuracy-per-parameter at exactly the tiny scale we care about** — but three gaps
keep it from being directly actionable: (1) it is unquantized floating point, so we don't know if
the accuracy holds under W1A8/A6/A4; (2) it never touches hardware, so the "45 pairwise dot
products at 10 constituents" cost (our thesis's central resource question) is untested in
LUT/DSP/latency terms; (3) their own "tiny LorentzNet" comparison (120 params, 0.907 acc) is the
authors' own cut-down reimplementation for a fair small-scale comparison, not the original
LorentzNet paper's 220k-param model — don't conflate the two when citing "LorentzNet at small
scale." The single most promising angle this paper opens for us: replacing our 10-constituent
inputs with a 10×10 (or 5×5, testing sensitivity) pairwise-dot-product matrix feeding *binary*
`LinEq2→2`-style aggregation layers is a well-specified, cheap experiment (see verdict table in
the design-document report this brief supports).
