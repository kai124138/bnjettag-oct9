# PELICAN: Permutation Equivariant and Lorentz Invariant or Covariant Aggregator Network for Particle Physics

- **Authors:** Alexander Bogatskiy, Timothy Hoffman, David W. Miller, Jan T. Offermann
- **Venue / Year:** NeurIPS 2022 Workshop on Machine Learning and the Physical Sciences; 2022.
  **Extended journal version:** "Explainable Equivariant Neural Networks for Particle Physics:
  PELICAN," JHEP 03 (2024) 113, arXiv:2307.16506 (submitted 2023-07-31, accepted 2024-02-25) —
  adds quark/gluon and 5-class multiclass results, 4-momentum regression, and explainability.
- **arXiv:** 2211.00454 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00454 (journal version: 2307.16506 —
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16506)
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.00454
- **Code:** https://github.com/abogatskiy/PELICAN
- **Topic area:** Jet tagging & transformers (Lorentz-equivariant aggregator, physics-native
  alternative to attention)

## TL;DR
Reduces each jet to the **N×N matrix of pairwise Minkowski dot products** `d_ij = p_i·p_j`
(the maximal Lorentz-invariant reduction of the input 4-momenta) and stacks
**permutation-equivariant linear aggregation** layers on top — no learned attention, no MLP over
concatenated raw features. On the standard top-tagging benchmark a **45k-parameter** PELICAN beats
every heavier competitor tested, including LorentzNet (220k params) and ParticleNet (498k params).

## Summary
PELICAN's central design move is to do the expensive, genuinely data-dependent
(activation×activation) computation exactly once, at the input: form the symmetric N×N matrix of
Minkowski inner products between all pairs of 4-momenta (this is provably the maximal set of
Lorentz invariants one can build from N four-vectors, up to a small set of extra terms needed for
parity/covariance). Every subsequent layer is a **permutation-equivariant linear map** on that
matrix (the 15-function basis of Maron et al., "Invariant and Equivariant Graph Networks," ICLR
2019), with channel mixing and occasional elementwise nonlinearities (ReLU) — i.e. ordinary
weight×activation arithmetic, no further pairwise recomputation. A learnable `(N/N̄)^α` rescaling
handles variable particle multiplicity across jets.

## Key methods / contributions
- **Input = N×N Minkowski dot-product matrix**, not raw 4-vectors or per-particle features —
  the "maximal reduction" argument means no invariant information is discarded relative to a
  fully generic (non-equivariant) network.
- **Eq2→2 layers**: full 15-basis-function permutation-equivariant linear aggregation over the
  N×N tensor (row/column/full sums, means, diagonal extraction, transposes, etc. — the complete
  basis for equivariant linear maps `R^{N×N}→R^{N×N}`).
- **Eq2→0 layer**: reduces to a permutation-invariant feature vector for the classification head.
- Extremely **parameter-efficient**: state-of-the-art top-tagging accuracy at 45k params, ~5×
  fewer than the next-best model in their comparison (Table 1, arXiv:2211.00454, via ar5iv fetch,
  accessed 2026-07-24).
- Journal version (2307.16506) adds quark/gluon tagging, a 5-class multiclass task, W-boson
  4-momentum regression inside boosted tops, and Grad-CAM-style explainability analysis of which
  aggregators/invariants drive predictions.

## Main results
Top-tagging benchmark (Kasieczka et al. reference dataset), Table 1 of arXiv:2211.00454 (ar5iv
fetch, accessed 2026-07-24):

| Architecture | Accuracy | AUC | 1/ε_B @ ε_S=0.3 | Params |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LGN (Lorentz Group Network, arXiv:2006.04780) | 0.929(1) | 0.964(14) | 424 ± 82 | 4.5k |
| PFN | 0.932 | 0.982 | 891 ± 18 | 82k |
| ResNeXt | 0.936 | 0.984 | 1122 ± 47 | 1.46M |
| ParticleNet | 0.938 | 0.985 | 1298 ± 46 | 498k |
| LorentzNet | 0.942 | 0.9868 | 2195 ± 173 | 220k |
| **PELICAN** | **0.9425(1)** | **0.9869(1)** | **2289 ± 204** | **45k** |

No FPGA/hls4ml/latency numbers anywhere in either version — accuracy-only, full floating-point
precision, GPU-trained/evaluated. Exact constituent-count truncation used for the headline 45k
model was **not independently confirmed this session** (the follow-up nanoPELICAN paper,
2310.16121, explicitly truncates to the 80 highest-pT constituents out of the dataset's ~200
max — plausibly the same convention, but not verified against PELICAN's own text directly).

## Relevance to BNJetTag
PELICAN is the cleanest published existence proof of the exact hardware-shaped pattern our
adversarial design doc is asking about: **one activation×activation step at the input (forming
the pairwise dot-product matrix), then everything else is weight×activation (linear aggregation) +
cheap elementwise nonlinearities.** At 10 constituents, the same construction would need only 45
off-diagonal (+10 diagonal self) Minkowski dot products, each a 4-multiply/3-add contraction — a
small, fixed, one-time cost, in principle binarizable/quantizable in every layer AFTER that first
step. Caveats for us: (1) no hardware study exists for PELICAN at any scale — the parameter
efficiency claim is unverified in LUT/DSP/latency terms; (2) it targets the standard top-tagging
binary-classification benchmark, not our 5-class HLS4ML LHC Jet dataset; (3) it uses one scalar
invariant (Minkowski dot product) per pair, not the four ParT-style invariants (ΔR, kT, z, m²);
(4) it has never been quantized, let alone binarized. See also `2310.16121_nanopelican.md` for the
same authors' own extreme-small-parameter follow-up (down to 11 params), which is the more
directly relevant data point for our ≤20k-param regime.
