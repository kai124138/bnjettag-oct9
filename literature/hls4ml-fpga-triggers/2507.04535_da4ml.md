# da4ml: Distributed Arithmetic for Real-time Neural Networks on FPGAs

- **Authors:** Chang Sun, Zhiqiang Que, Vladimir Loncar, Wayne Luk, Maria Spiropulu
- **Venue / Year:** ACM Trans. Reconfigurable Technology and Systems (TRETS) 19(1), Art. 13
  (March 2026), 28 pp. arXiv v2 posted 24 Apr 2026 (v1 Jul 2025).
- **arXiv:** 2507.04535 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04535
- **DOI:** 10.1145/3777387 — https://doi.org/10.1145/3777387
- **Code:** https://github.com/calad0i/da4ml (LGPL-3.0); docs https://calad0i.github.io/da4ml/
- **Topic area:** hls4ml & FPGA triggers — this is *the engine* behind hls4ml
  `Strategy='distributed_arithmetic'` (hls4ml pins `da4ml<0.6`; we ran 0.5.2).
- **Accessed:** 2026-07-24 (PDF read via pdftotext; repo via GitHub API + raw source).

## TL;DR
da4ml is a CMVM (constant matrix-vector multiply) optimizer that turns a fixed weight matrix
into a multiplier-free adder graph, killing all DSPs and cutting LUTs "by up to a third" on
*realistic, heterogeneously-quantized* HGQ networks. Its win comes from two mechanisms —
CSD digit reduction (~1/3 of digits on average) and pairwise two-term common-subexpression
elimination — **both of which have essentially nothing to work with on binary ±1 matrices**
(1 signed digit per weight, no shared two-term subexpressions). The paper contains **no
binary/ternary weight test anywhere**; the only extreme-low-bit statement is that for a
network with *1-bit inputs* they *skip da4ml entirely* in favor of conditional-accumulation
logic. Our measured +34% LUT / 3.7× FF inflation on our ±1 denses is off the end of their
tested range and is consistent with their own stated failure mode ("trivial decomposition").

## What they actually did (setup, reproducible)
- **Algorithm (§3–§4):** minimize LUTs for `y^T = x^T M` as an adder tree under a delay
  constraint `dc` (extra adder depth beyond `depth_min = ceil(log2(d_in))`). Cost of one
  `a ± (b<<s)` node = `max(bw_a, bw_b+s) − min(0,s) + 1` full/half adders (Eq. 1).
  **Hybrid two stages:** (1) *graph decomposition* `M = M1·M2` — columns of M are graph
  vertices, edge weight = `min(#nonzero-CSD-digits(v_i ± v_j))`, an approximate **Prim MST**
  from a zero root captures inter-column correlation; (2) **CSD + greedy two-term CSE** —
  repeatedly implements the *most common* `a ± b<<s` subexpression (not min-conflict, "less
  than 2% [worse]"), frequency weighted by operand bit-overlap. `O(N^2)`, ~10^5× faster than
  Hcmvm [4]. **Exact — no approximation** of the trained weights.
- **Emission:** two paths. (i) hls4ml plug-in: emits combinational adder-tree C++, **Vitis**
  inserts pipeline registers to meet timing (`ReuseFactor=1`, Dense/EinsumDense/Conv1D-2D;
  hls4ml 1.3.0 docs add MHA). (ii) **Standalone**: lowers to DAIS IR (SSA), emits **Verilog
  or VHDL**, and *inserts a register between DAIS ops* by a greedy local `latency_cutoff`
  (every adder at 1 GHz, every 5 adders at 200 MHz) — the register-heavy path.
- **Benchmarks:** random 4-bit & 8-bit matrices; HGQ-trained JSC jet MLP (16-64-32-16-16-5),
  SVHN LeNet, muon-tracking MLP, MLP-Mixer particle tagger. Target VU13P (xcvu13p), Vitis/
  Vivado 2023.2, II=1. All weights are **HGQ heterogeneous multi-bit**; none binary/ternary.

## The numbers that matter to us (with source)
- Realistic-network LUT reduction: JSC jet ~10% (their Table 5), SVHN ~1/4 (Table 7), muon
  ~10% (Table 8), MLP-Mixer marginal (Table 9); **DSP → 0 in every case**. The headline
  "up to a third" (Abstract; §2.2) is the *upper* end, not typical. **These are top-1
  accuracy / MSE-resolution benchmarks, not our 5-class macro-OvR AUC, and none is binary —
  no legitimate direct comparison to our numbers exists.**
- **HLS-path FF is NOT inflated** in their realistic tables: Table 5 DA FF 1,502 vs Latency
  1,497 (≈equal); Table 7 20,048 vs 27,853 and Table 8 5,547 vs 6,043 (**DA lower**). FF
  blowup only appears on the *standalone RTL* path at 200 MHz (Table 10: 3,113 vs 1,502).
  → **Correction flag for our internal note:** our measured 3.7× FF was the *HLS* path, where
  Vitis (not da4ml) inserts registers; the "registers everything" discipline lives in the
  *standalone* path. Our blowup is Vitis pipelining a *larger ±1 adder graph*, not da4ml's
  own register insertion. Same conclusion (binary is pathological), corrected mechanism.
- Their §4.3: on uncorrelated columns "the algorithm would usually produce trivial
  decomposition, where M1 is a shuffled form of M and M2 is a shuffled identity" — exactly
  our forensic "0 of 373 shareable output columns."

## What we can steal
- The `dc` (delay-constraint) knob: `dc=2` is their chosen sweet spot; `dc=-1` (no
  constraint) gave the lowest LUT on random matrices — worth a scan if we ever want da4ml on
  the non-binary attention/pooling sublayers (where weights aren't ±1 and DSPs are nonzero).
- Standalone RTL path as a *synthesis-time/RAM* escape hatch (their MLP-Mixer: ~17 h + ~70 GB
  HLS → ~26 min + ~10 GB Verilog) — relevant to our mulder OOM/day-long block jobs, orthogonal
  to the LUT question.
- Their Table 13 is a ready-made competitor board (NeuraLUT-Assemble, TreeLUT, PolyLUT,
  LogicNets, DWN) for the JSC jet task — a baseline set we could cite/position against.

## What threatens us — and what does not
- **Does NOT scoop the thesis.** da4ml is a *general* CMVM optimizer whose premise (CSD digit
  sharing + CSE) *vanishes* at ±1; our DSP=0 story is orthogonal (both hit DSP=0, but binary
  gets there for free via sign-flip, and da4ml *inflates* LUT/FF on ±1). It does not show
  activations can go lower, and it says nothing about binary vs ternary accuracy.
- **The real risk is rhetorical:** a referee may ask "did you just use da4ml wrong?" Our
  answer is the measured negative + the paper's own trivial-decomposition clause + the absence
  of any binary weight test in their work. This paper is the citation that makes our adversarial
  "binary-native compiler" gap real: da4ml searches (a) MST inter-column correlation and (b)
  pairwise two-term CSE — it does **not** attempt subset-sum/block precompute, the
  `2·Σ(+1) − Σ_all` affine ±1 offset, or cross-layer Wq/Wk/Wv fusion (see the dossier's
  capability table). Those are precisely the ±1-native wins outside its search space.

## How we'd cite it
"Distributed-arithmetic CMVM optimization (da4ml [Sun2026]) reduces LUTs by up to a third on
heterogeneously-quantized networks by sharing signed-digit subexpressions; on binary ±1
matrices this premise degenerates — each weight is a single CSD digit — and we measure a LUT
*increase*, motivating a binary-native adder-graph compiler."
