## One-line claim
hls4ml can be extended with MHA, SoftMax and layer-normalization layers so that any
TensorFlow-built transformer converts automatically, and three ~3–9k-parameter physics
transformers then run on a VU13P with latency "less than 2 µs".

## THE GOVERNING QUESTION: which axis is reused? — verdict (a), with an unclaimed dash of (c)

**(a) is what they expose, measure, and claim.** §6.2 defines reuse verbatim:

> "Parallelization is another key aspect of FPGA optimization in `hls4ml`, coordinated mainly
> through a parameter mechanism known as "reuse". This parameter represents the number of
> multiplication operations to be performed by each digital signal processing (DSP) block for a
> given matrix multiplication."
> "In cases where the reuse factor is set to 1, leading to a fully parallel configuration, each
> multiplication is handled independently by a separate DSP … As we increase the reuse factor,
> the number of required DSPs decreases."

That is textbook hls4ml `reuse_factor` = **weight-multiplier sharing**, the knob our repo
measured dead for ±1 (LUT bit-identical RF8→10). Their entire tradeoff study is R ∈ {1, 2, 4}.

**The "Resource strategy at the top level" sentence is not folding.** Full quote (§6.2):

> "we employed a layered strategy: smaller components like the dense layer, normalization layer,
> and SoftMax layer within the MHA block adhered to the latency strategy, thus producing outputs
> every cycle. On the other hand, the top level of the transformer model, due to its larger size,
> invariably followed the resource strategy. In this manner, the design is optimized for low
> resource utilization by leveraging the same hardware to execute operations across multiple
> stages."

"the same hardware … across multiple stages" is the hls4ml Resource-strategy *mult kernel*
(loop over RF blocks) — still (a), with at most a weak flavour of (b). It is **not** (c).

**But the fixed MHA dataflow *is* token-serial — a real, unnamed instance of (c).** §4.1:

> Stage 1: "Inside this stage, a matrix times a vector operation is performed at each time step.
> In other words, the stage 1 itself is also a pipelined process, which would produce **one row
> of the output matrix at each time step**, with each row representing a time step of data."
> Stage 3: "one row of the relevance score matrix … is loaded. This row is then multiplied by the
> V matrix to generate a new row in the output attention vector. **This process is repeated until
> all rows of the score matrix have been processed.**"
> Stage 4: "These output vectors are loaded **row by row, aligning with the temporal sequencing of
> the data** … The linear layer is also pipelined; it reads one row of data and outputs one row at
> a time."

Corroborated quantitatively by their own Tables 2–4: **II ≈ 2·seq_len + const** across three
sequence lengths — seq 15 → II 49 (= 2·15+19), seq 50 → II 119 (= 2·50+19), seq 100 → II 212
(= 2·100+12). The token axis sets the initiation interval. Second corroboration: the 9,135-param
B-tagging model uses ~50% of the VU13P's DSPs; a spatial unroll over 15 positions would need
~15× the multipliers (far beyond the device), so the weight-multiplier array is demonstrably
shared across sequence positions.

**Why this is nonetheless not our mechanism — four specifics:**
1. **"fold", "time-multiplex" and "token" appear nowhere** in either version (grep-verified).
   The token-serial structure is never named, claimed, or presented as a resource mechanism.
2. **No knob, no ablation, no curve.** seq_len is a property of the model, not a schedule
   parameter. There is no token-axis area-vs-II result anywhere in the paper. The only swept
   parameter is R.
3. **The attention core is explicitly NOT area-folded over tokens.** §4.1: K vectors are "loaded
   into a two-dimensional register, allowing simultaneous retrieval of all K data points" and the
   V vectors are "stored in a fully accessible register" — full seq_len × d register arrays. Area
   still grows with sequence length, which is exactly why their seq-100 model does not fit (below).
4. **Weights live in DSPs/BRAM, not as constants in LUT logic.** The constant-weight,
   hand-emitted, DSP-free premise our fold is built on is absent.

**Verdict on (a)/(b)/(c): (a) for everything measured and claimed; an unclaimed, unmeasured,
non-tunable structural instance of (c) in the fixed MHA dataflow; not (b).**

## What they actually did (setup, reproducible)
- **Tools:** **Vivado HLS 2019.2** (not Vitis), device **Xilinx UltraScale+ VU13P**, one device for
  all models "to ensure a fair comparison". **hls4ml version never stated.**
- **Quantization: QKeras**, not HGQ/HGQ2. Both PTQ and QAT. They extended QKeras themselves —
  "the current version of Qkeras does not support certain layers, specifically MHA, SoftMax, and
  Layer Normalization. To address this gap, we enhanced the package by incorporating quantizers
  for each of these layers." **Uniform precision across all layers** ("we kept the same precision
  across all layers"); accumulator pinned at **10 bits including sign**; fractional bits swept.
  Chosen integer bits: engine 6 (PTQ & QAT), B-tagging **10 (PTQ) / 6 (QAT)**, GW 6 (both).
- **MHA = 4 sequential pipeline stages** (fig. 4): (1) QKV projection, matrix×vector per time step,
  outputs into stacked FIFOs ("The number of FIFO memories depends on the number of reuse factor
  and the number of outputs of the previous layer"); (2) Q·Kᵀ → scale by √d_k (a compile-time
  constant) → softmax, with a V matrix reshape; (3) score·V; (4) head concat + output projection.
- **SoftMax:** replaced hls4ml's `Sᵢ = (Σⱼ e^(zⱼ−zᵢ))⁻¹` (k² ops) with **`Sᵢ = (Σⱼ e^zⱼ)⁻¹ × e^zᵢ`
  (k ops)**, in 3 stages: exp LUT → sum + inversion LUT held in a register → element-wise multiply.
- **LayerNorm:** 5 stages — mean, deviation-from-mean, variance, `1/√var` **by LUT**, then γ·x+β
  via a dot-product unit.
- **Strategy split:** `Latency` for dense/norm/softmax inside the MHA block; `Resource` at the
  transformer top level. Reuse also used to partition arrays into **BRAM**.
- **Models (their table 1) — heads, head dim, and FFN width are never stated:**

  | | Engine | **B-tagging** | GW |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Seq. length | 50 | **15** | 100 |
  | Input vec. size | 1 | **6** | 2 |
  | Transformer blocks | 3 | **3** | 2 |
  | Hidden vec. size | 16 | **64** | 32 |
  | Output vec. size | 2 | **3** | 1 |
  | Trainable params | 3,244 | **9,135** | 3,394 |

- **B-tagging data:** CMS open-data tt̄ sample (opendata.cern.ch/record/204), 7 TeV, **3-class**
  b / c / light. **Not** the HLS4ML LHC Jet 5-class dataset we use.
- **Scaling:** none. No scaling study, no discussion of fitting larger models. Future work is
  masking and sparse dense layers.

## The numbers that matter to us (with source, and what they are)

**LEAD FINDING — their AUC is not an AUC in our sense, and the B-tagging model has no reported
physics performance at all.** §6.1, verbatim:

> "It is crucial to understand that these AUC metrics are derived from **comparing the outputs of
> the Keras/QKeras model and the hls4ml model, rather than comparing the hls4ml output with the
> ground truth of the dataset**, because we are primarily interested in the capability of hls4ml
> to replicate the output of the Keras and QKeras model accurately."

So figs. 9/10/11 ("ration AUCs") are an **hls4ml-vs-Keras fidelity ratio**, not tagging
performance. The only ground-truth numbers in the paper are engine **98% validation accuracy**
and GW **AUC 97.8%**, both quoted for the Keras model, not the FPGA. **For the B-tagging model
there is no accuracy or AUC against truth anywhere in the paper.** Fig. 10 caption also concedes
"The QAT's result remains invalid at 0.5 until 6 fractional bits." → **No number in this paper is
comparable to our macro-OvR AUC** (different dataset, different class count, different metric
definition). Nothing of theirs may be placed next to our 0.8902.

**Latency / II (their tables 2–4), B-tagging row in bold:**

| model (seq) | R | clk (ns) | II (cyc) | latency (cyc) | latency (µs) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Engine (50) PTQ=QAT | 1 / 2 / 4 | 7.423 / 4.367 / 4.367 | 119 / 218 / 318 | 257 / 456 / 756 | 1.908 / 2.280 / 3.780 |
| **B-tag (15) PTQ** | **1 / 2 / 4** | **6.577 / 6.215 / 4.723** | **49 / 65 / 100** | **269 / 449 / 768** | **2.077 / 3.467 / 5.853** |
| **B-tag (15) QAT** | **1 / 2 / 4** | **6.568 / 6.210 / 4.722** | **48 / 63 / 99** | **266 / 445 / 767** | **2.055 / 3.440 / 5.848** |
| GW (100) PTQ | 1 / 2 / 4 | 6.577 / 6.215 / 4.723 | 212 / 412 / 612 | 537 / 1035 / 1835 | 3.532 / 6.433 / 9.175 |

Note the abstract's "latency less than 2 µs" is true for **exactly one row** (engine, R1, 1.908 µs);
the CMS B-tagging model is 2.077 µs at best and the GW model 3.532 µs. **The headline number is an
abstract-only overstatement relative to the body tables.**

**Three data-quality flags on those tables (ours, not theirs):**
- `latency(µs) ≠ cycles × clk(ns)` for 5 of 12 rows. B-tag PTQ R1: 269 × 6.577 = **1.769 µs**, not
  the reported 2.077; R4: 768 × 4.723 = **3.627 µs**, not 5.853. (Engine R1, GW R1 and GW R2 do
  reconcile exactly.)
- The clk column of table 3 (PTQ) and table 4 (PTQ) is **bit-identical** (6.577 / 6.215 / 4.723) for
  two different models — implausible.
- Table 2's PTQ and QAT rows are bit-identical in **every** column.
  ⇒ Treat their timing numbers as indicative only; do not quote a single one as precise.

**Resources — percentages only; there is not one absolute LUT/FF/DSP count in the paper**, and
**no BRAM data at all** despite BRAM being listed among the "quantified" categories. Stage:
"**All results are from Vivado mapped designs**" — i.e. past HLS csynth estimation, through Vivado
synthesis/mapping; *post-place-and-route is never claimed*. Read off figs. 12–14:

| model | LUT % | FF % | DSP % |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engine (fig. 12) | 14–22 (R2/R4) · **38–70 (R1)** | 8–12 (R1) · 16–23 (R2/R4) | 21–23 / 41–43 / **56–57** |
| **B-tagging (fig. 13)** | **15–38** | **6–20** | **18–19 / 31–34 / 46–57** |
| GW (fig. 14) | 40–45 (R4) · 50–80 (R2) · **145–280 (R1)** | 25–52 | 33–34 / 45–46 / **56–57** |

**The GW model does not fit.** At R=1 its LUT usage runs ~145% → ~280% of the VU13P across the
precision scan — yet table 4 still reports a 3.532 µs latency for that configuration. This is
never acknowledged in the text.

Converting with VU13P totals (1,728,000 LUT / 3,456,000 FF / 12,288 DSP) — **our arithmetic on
their percentage plots, not their numbers**: B-tagging ≈ **260k–660k LUT, 210k–690k FF,
2,200–7,000 DSP** for 9,135 parameters. Two further anomalies: all three models sit at ~56–57%
DSP at R1 despite param counts of 3,244 / 9,135 / 3,394 (**DSP does not track parameter count**);
and **FF *rises* with reuse** for engine and B-tagging, directly contradicting their own sentence
"reuse can be manipulated to reduce FF and LUT counts while incurring a latency penalty" — LUT
falls with R, FF does not.

**Legitimacy of comparison to us:** none of the above is comparable to our numbers. Different tool
(Vivado HLS 2019.2 vs our Vitis 2023.2), different reporting stage (their Vivado-mapped vs our
csynth, where we measured csynth over-estimating LUT by ≈2.3–2.5×), different quantization stack
(QKeras vs HGQ2), different weights (multi-bit in DSPs vs our ±1 in logic), different dataset and
task. The one structurally meaningful cross-read is **qualitative**: their II grows with sequence
length, and their long-sequence model exceeds device capacity.

## What we can steal
- **The softmax reformulation** `Sᵢ = (Σⱼ e^zⱼ)⁻¹ · e^zᵢ` — k ops instead of k², exp LUT + one
  inversion LUT in a register, 3 stages. It is **linear in sequence length**, so it composes
  cleanly with a token fold; a concrete, citable design for the softmax we currently treat as a
  synthesis line item.
- **The LayerNorm 5-stage decomposition** with `1/√var` by LUT — directly reusable if SubLN ever
  goes back into hardware.
- **The inter-stage FIFO pattern**, with FIFO count = f(reuse factor, previous layer output width) —
  the buffering shape a token-folded datapath needs between stages.
- **A free motivation paragraph:** their seq-100 model blowing past 100% VU13P LUT at R=1 is
  *published, independent evidence that the token axis is the scaling wall for hls4ml
  transformers* — precisely the gap our fold fills. Use it.
- **An independent datapoint that token-rate II is the natural regime** (II ≈ 2T + const at
  T = 15/50/100), which makes our II = T = 10 contract look like a tightening of established
  practice rather than an exotic choice.
- **Not stealable:** any baseline run. Their dataset (CMS open-data tt̄, 3-class) is not ours.

## What threatens us
- **The novelty claim must be narrowed — this is the real cost of this paper.** We can no longer
  write "nobody has processed transformer tokens serially through a shared datapath in hls4ml."
  They do, and II ≈ 2T + const proves it. What survives, and is what we must say instead: (i)
  token-serial *as a tunable schedule under a fixed-II contract* (II = T, `rewind`, budget check);
  (ii) token folding *as a measured area mechanism with an ablation* — they report **no** token-axis
  area result at all, we have arm A 9.3× and arm C 18.0× LUT; (iii) folding a **constant-weight,
  DSP-free ±1** datapath; (iv) folding the **whole** datapath **including the attention core**,
  where they keep K and V fully register-partitioned so their area still grows with T. A narrowing,
  not a kill — but our previous framing was too strong and should be corrected in COMPILER.md.
- **Priority and politics.** They are first to publish MHA/softmax/LayerNorm in hls4ml on a VU13P
  (Sep 2024 preprint, JINST Apr 2025), and Loncar / Harris / Govorkova overlap the hls4ml core
  team. A referee will expect this cited and positioned against, not ignored.
- **Not a threat on the thesis.** DSP=0: they spend ~50% of the VU13P's DSPs on a 9k-param model —
  this paper makes our DSP=0 story stronger, not weaker. Accuracy: no comparable number exists.
  Activation depth: uniform precision with a pinned 10-bit accumulator; they never push activations
  low, so nothing here says activations can go lower than we thought. Binary vs ternary: nothing
  1-bit or 2-bit appears anywhere, so the binary-only framing is untouched.
- **Unresolvable from the paper** (state as unknown if we ever need it): head count, head dim, FFN
  width, BRAM usage, hls4ml version, whether the MHA implementation was ever upstreamed (no code
  link; hls4ml issue #1020 "LLM support, attention head and transformer architecture support" has
  been open since 2024-06 and upstream attention arrived later by a different route, EinsumDense).

## How we'd cite it
"Transformer support in hls4ml [Jiang2025] demonstrated multi-head attention, softmax and
layer-normalization layers on a VU13P for three transformers of 3–9k parameters, exposing
hls4ml's DSP-sharing reuse factor as the only resource knob and reporting initiation intervals
that scale with sequence length (49, 119 and 212 cycles at sequence lengths 15, 50 and 100); those
designs consume roughly half of the device's DSPs, and the longest-sequence model exceeds the
VU13P LUT capacity — motivating an explicit token-axis folding schedule over a constant-weight
binary datapath."

## Verdict: **essential**
The closest prior art to the fit campaign's central mechanism. It does not scoop token folding as
a *method*, but it does establish token-serial MHA dataflow in hls4ml as published fact, which
forces a precise rewording of our novelty claim. It is also the cleanest published evidence that
the token axis is where hls4ml transformers run out of device.
