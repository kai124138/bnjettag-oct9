## TL;DR
Instead of a multiply–accumulate per weight, each neuron becomes a small function of a few
multi-bit inputs (a "logic LUT", L-LUT) that is trained as a tiny tanh MLP with ordinary tensor
operations — so training runs at GPU speed (≈200× faster than NeuraLUT-Assemble) — and compiled
to physical K-input FPGA LUTs. A differentiable LUT-count surrogate replaces EBOPs for these
layers, per-element HGQ quantization (with zero-bit pruning) applies to every L-LUT input and
output, and LUT-Dense / LUT-Conv layers mix freely with ordinary HGQ dense layers in one model.
Result on the 16-feature jet task: 5,667 LUT / 0 DSP / 9.2 ns at the same accuracy as HGQ at
10,182 LUT / 36.3 ns; on the 64-particle × 16-feature task a LUT-native GNN at 39,765 LUT versus
244,515 for HGQ, at −1.4 accuracy points.

## Method
- **L-LUT relaxation.** An MLP layer is relaxed so that each (input, output) pair carries its own
  function L-LUT_{i,j}(a_j); the neuron output is the sum over j. Strictly a superset of the MLP
  (universal approximation carries over). Each L-LUT is realized during training as a 2-layer
  tanh MLP, vectorized as an einsum, so a whole layer is one GEMM plus an activation.
- **Resource surrogate.** For an m-bit-input, n-bit-output L-LUT on X-input primitives: 2^(m−X)·n
  LUT units when m ≥ Y, ≈ (m/Y)·2^(Y−X)·n when m < Y, with Y the smallest fracturable LUT size
  (5 on current Xilinx parts). This enters HGQ's differentiable resource loss alongside EBOPs;
  input and output widths of each L-LUT are individually quantized and can go to 0 bits (pruned).
- **Hybrid models.** Feature extraction with conventional (matmul) HGQ layers, heads with
  LUT-Dense, or the reverse; a unified compiler IR (Alkaid) handles both operation types.
- **Hardware.** VU13P, RTL via Alkaid, out-of-context Vivado; clocks 122–984 MHz depending on the
  design (JSC HLF at 851.8 MHz, 4-cycle latency).

## Relevance to BNJetTag
1. **It is orthogonal to weight binarization and nobody has crossed the two.** HGQ-LUT removes
   multiplies by making neurons table lookups; we remove them by making weights ±1. Both give
   0 DSP. Theirs also deletes most of the *operations* (0-bit pruning); ours keeps every one of the
   133,024 MACs as an adder. This is the framework difference behind our LUT count
   (`docs/reports/research-reset-2026-09-05.md` §2–§3).
2. **No transformer support.** The paper names transformers as future work; attention's act × act
   products have no L-LUT form (both operands are activations). Our N² attention block would stay
   as it is under any LUT-native head.
3. **The hybrid pattern is the transferable idea:** LUT-Dense for the low-fan-in parts (the
   5-class head, possibly the FFN after pruning) and matmul layers elsewhere. A test needs Alkaid,
   not hls4ml.
4. **Their resource surrogate has no binary-weight term** — a 1-bit-weight × m-bit-activation
   term in EBOPs is m, the same as any adder; nothing in their cost model rewards ±1 over a
   pruned 2-bit weight.

## Cite it for
The state of LUT-aware training in 2026; the HGQ vs LUT-native Pareto on the jet datasets; the

by omission, that transformers and binary weights remain outside the LUT-native literature.
