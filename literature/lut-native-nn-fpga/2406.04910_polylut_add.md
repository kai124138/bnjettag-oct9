# PolyLUT-Add: FPGA-based LUT Inference with Wide Inputs

- **Authors:** Binglei Lou, Richard Rademacher, David Boland, Philip H. W. Leong — (per PolyLUT/NeuraLUT-Assemble reference lists; Leong's group, Univ. of Sydney).
- **Venue / Year:** FPL 2024 (34th Int'l Conf. on Field-Programmable Logic and Applications), 2024, pp. 149–155, doi: 10.1109/FPL64840.2024.00029.
- **arXiv:** 2406.04910 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04910
- **Topic area:** LUT-native neural networks for FPGAs

## TL;DR
A cheap way to widen effective fan-in without paying PolyLUT's exponential LUT cost directly: sum the outputs of **A** independent PolyLUT sub-neurons (each still small-fan-in, `F` inputs) through an **A-input adder**, moving batch-norm/quantization to *after* the sum rather than inside each sub-neuron. This decouples "total effective fan-in" (`A × F`) from "per-LUT truth-table size" (still `2^(βF)` per sub-neuron, small), trading a modest amount of extra adder/FF logic for meaningfully higher accuracy at the same F.

## (a) Mechanism
Same family pattern: fixed-topology, sparsity-and-quantization-constrained network, trained end-to-end via QAT (Brevitas), then each `F`-input sub-neuron enumerated into an L-LUT exactly as in PolyLUT; the **novel piece is architectural** (an adder combining A sub-neuron outputs before batchnorm/activation), not a change to the train→enumerate pipeline. **Training-vs-compiling nuance flagged as UNVERIFIED this session:** an earlier automated extraction pass suggested PolyLUT-Add "uses existing pre-trained PolyLUT networks" rather than training end-to-end; this conflicts with the consistent "train the specific constrained topology from scratch, then enumerate" pattern independently confirmed by direct primary-text reads of LogicNets, PolyLUT, and NeuraLUT-Assemble's own characterization of the whole family (see NeuraLUT-Assemble brief, §II.E). The end-to-end-QAT reading is far more consistent with the rest of the literature; treat "reuses a frozen pretrained PolyLUT checkpoint" as **UNVERIFIED / likely incorrect** pending a direct re-read of PolyLUT-Add's own methodology section.

## (b) What it explicitly does NOT do
- MLP-only; no attention/sequence-model mention.
- Per-sub-neuron fan-in `F` stays small (e.g., F=3 for the JSC-XL configurations below); the "Add" trick widens *effective* connectivity, it does not remove the underlying exponential-in-F truth-table wall for any single sub-neuron.
- Same family-wide absence of any externally-pretrained-checkpoint ingestion path.

## (c) Exact published jet-tagging numbers (cross-validated across 3 independent primary/near-primary sources this session: PolyLUT-Add's own paper via WebFetch extraction, AmigoLUT's Table 4, NeuraLUT-Assemble's Table IV — all agree)

JSC-XL family, target device **xcvu9p-flgb2104-2-i**:

| Config | Degree D | Effective fan-in (F×A) | Accuracy | LUT | FF | F_max | Latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| JSC-XL PolyLUT (baseline) | 1 | F=3 | 74.5% | 2^15 (~32.8k) | — | 235 MHz | 5 cyc |
| JSC-XL PolyLUT-Add | 1 | 3×2 | 75.1% | ~2^15×2+2^12 | — | 235 MHz | 5 cyc |
| JSC-XL PolyLUT-Add, D=3 ("XL-Add2") | 3 | — | **75%** | **36,484** | 1,209 | 315 MHz | **16 ns** |
| JSC-XL PolyLUT, D=4 (comparison) | 4 | — | 75% | 236,541 | 2,775 | 235 MHz | 21 ns |

Smaller/lower-accuracy operating point (matches AmigoLUT Table 4 and the LUT-NN survey's "JSC-M Lite-Add2"):

| Config | Accuracy | LUT | FF | Latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PolyLUT-Add (small) | 72% | 895 | 1,649 | 4 ns |

I.e. PolyLUT-Add reaches PolyLUT-D4's 75% accuracy at **6.5× fewer LUTs** (36,484 vs. 236,541) and similar latency (16 ns vs. 21 ns) — a genuine Pareto improvement over plain PolyLUT at the high-accuracy JSC operating point.

## Verdict
**OCCUPIES** the same solved MLP/JSC space, one more Pareto-efficient point on the family's curve. **ORTHOGONAL** to compiling a given trained transformer — architecture is still hand-designed for LUT-mapping before training starts, no attention/sequence capability.
