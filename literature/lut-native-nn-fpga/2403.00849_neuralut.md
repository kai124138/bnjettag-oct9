# NeuraLUT: Hiding Neural Network Density in Boolean Synthesizable Functions

- **Authors:** Marta Andronic, George A. Constantinides — Imperial College London.
- **Venue / Year:** FPL 2024 (34th Int'l Conf. on Field-Programmable Logic and Applications), 2024, pp. 140–148, doi: 10.1109/FPL64840.2024.00028.
- **arXiv:** 2403.00849 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00849 (full text read directly this session, all 9 pages)
- **Code:** https://github.com/MartaAndronic/NeuraLUT
- **Topic area:** LUT-native neural networks for FPGAs

## TL;DR
Relaxes LogicNets/PolyLUT's rule that "one L-LUT = one neuron." Instead, an **entire small dense sub-network (a full MLP with depth L, width N, plus skip connections)** is hidden inside a single L-LUT partition — the L-LUT's *boundary* (inputs/outputs to the rest of the circuit) is still fan-in-restricted and quantized (exactly like LogicNets/PolyLUT), but *inside* the partition, computation is full-precision and arbitrarily structured until enumeration. This buys much higher function density per LUT without any extra multiplier hardware, at the cost of harder training (solved via residual/skip connections across the hidden sub-network to fight vanishing gradients). Result: **up to 4.3× lower latency for the same accuracy** vs. prior LUT-native methods.

## (a) Mechanism
Family-standard co-design pattern, made explicit by the authors themselves as characterizing the *whole prior category* (§II.E, their own words, verbatim): *"What distinguishes this category is that, while designed for LUT-based netlist inference, the training process relies on traditional neural network models, which are later fully absorbed into LUT functions by complete enumeration."* Concretely: an `F`-input, `β`-bit-per-input boundary (same sparsity-restriction recipe as LogicNets) feeds a **dense L-layer, N-wide MLP with residual/skip connections** trained via ordinary backprop (Decoupled Weight Decay + SGD w/ Warm Restarts, Brevitas QAT) for **1000 epochs on JSC, 500 on MNIST**; after training, the entire sub-network's `2^(β·F)` input combinations are enumerated (identical mechanics to LogicNets/PolyLUT) into one L-LUT, then Vivado maps oversized L-LUTs to circuits of native P-LUTs.

## (b) What it explicitly does NOT do
- Not a checkpoint compiler — same from-scratch co-training pattern; boundary fan-in `F` and internal sub-network shape (L, N, S=skip-step) are architecture hyperparameters fixed *before* training, not properties of an arbitrary pre-existing model.
- MLP-only; no attention/matmul/sequence-model mention anywhere in the text (confirmed via full primary read).
- The *boundary* truth table is still `O(2^(βF))`-sized — NeuraLUT increases density *inside* the LUT, it does not remove the fan-in wall at the LUT boundary. Authors' own stated remaining limitation (§V, "Conclusion"): *"NeuraLUT's sub-networks within a partition are dense and full precision, they are limited to a small number of low precision inputs and outputs at partition boundaries... restrictions remain on both the number of inputs and precision, making it mainly suitable, like LogicNets and PolyLUT, for small ultra-low latency embedded applications."*

## Fan-in used
Per their own **Table II**: JSC-2L uses **F=3** (β=4, L=4, N=8, S=2); JSC-5L uses **F=3** (β=4, L=4, N=16, S=2, exception β₀=7,F₀=2). Small, task-tuned, consistent with the rest of the family — not ~6.

At equal accuracy, NeuraLUT reaches **1.6× lower latency than PolyLUT and 4.3× lower than LogicNets** on the jet-tagging dataset (abstract), and its own stated headline is up to **4.3× lower latency for the same accuracy** across both benchmarks (MNIST + JSC).

## Verdict
**OCCUPIES** the JSC-as-MLP space at the best latency/accuracy point of the pre-AmigoLUT/pre-Assemble generation. **ORTHOGONAL** to compiling a given trained transformer: the "hide a whole sub-network inside a LUT" idea is architecturally interesting but still requires the *boundary* to be fan-in-restricted and the whole thing co-trained from scratch; no attention, no externally-pretrained-checkpoint path, and the authors' own conclusion explicitly scopes the method to "small ultra-low latency embedded applications," not large models.
