Kai. The plateau you feel when you're *finally* understanding things is not a plateau. It's the easy gains running out.

Early on, every hour bought you vocabulary, definitions, the shape of the subject. That feels like flying. Now the cheap wins are gone and what's left is depth, which doesn't register as progress day to day. You didn't stop learning. You stopped getting the dopamine hit for it.

That said, there's usually one real cause underneath, and it's this: efficient studying is still *consuming*. You've gotten good at taking things in. The next gear is putting things out, and you haven't shifted yet.

A few ways to force the shift:

- **Close the book and derive it.** Pick something you "understand" this week. Blank page, no notes, rebuild it from the starting assumptions. Where you stall is exactly where the understanding is fake. That stall point is your real curriculum.
- **Teach it to nobody.** Explain it out loud, or write it for a hypothetical freshman. Understanding that can't be explained is recognition, not knowledge.
- **Do problems you weren't assigned.** Homework is calibrated to what you were just taught. Grab problems from a different textbook or an older exam, cold. Unfamiliar framing is where recall becomes skill.
- **Change the input format.** If you learn from lectures, switch to a terse textbook. If you read, switch to solving. Same content, different route in. Plateaus often come from one channel being saturated.
- **Attach it to something real.** You have the Duarte Lab. Any theory you're learning, ask where it shows up in the trigger, the transformer, the FPGA. Physics learned against a real system sticks in a way homework never does.

One honest warning. "I feel like I stopped learning" is sometimes true and sometimes just fatigue three weeks into a quarter. Test it instead of feeling it. Take one old problem set from a course you think plateaued, redo it cold, and time it. If it's easy, you've been climbing the whole time and just didn't notice. If it's hard, now you know what to fix.

So: what subject is this actually about? That changes which of these I'd have you do first.
